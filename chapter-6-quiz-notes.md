## Intro: Saint Godric
- 12th-c. **English** merchant (peddler → ships) who gave everything away and became a **hermit** = symbol of Europe's tension between **commerce and religion**.

## Timeline anchors (margins)
**1203–04 Fourth Crusade** → **1215 Magna Carta** → **1265 first Parliament** → **Louis IX** (Saint Louis, king of France) → **1338–1453 Hundred Years' War** → **1347–1380 Black Death** → **1380 expulsion of Tatars from Russia** → **1453 fall of Constantinople**.

## 6.1 Eastern Europe: Byzantine Decline
- **1204 — Fourth Crusade:** crusaders **sacked Constantinople** (a Christian city!) → East-West Christian relations poisoned; Byzantium restored later but **never recovered**.
- **Ottomans** (Turkic peoples, **converted to Islam** in Central Asia) formed a kingdom east of Constantinople in the 14th c.; **1382 crossed the Dardanelles into the Balkans, defeated Serbia** (fully absorbed in the 15th c.).
- **1453 — Ottomans capture Constantinople = end of the Byzantine Empire.**

## 6.2 Mongol Attack on Eastern Europe
- **1237–1242** — two Mongol invasions subdued Russian cities; Russians called them **"Tatars"** ("the accursed raw-eating Tatars").
- Mongols set up a capital; **local Russian rulers kept** but paid tribute and attended the Mongol court. **Trade lapsed in Western Russia.**
- Russian culture barely changed (Mongols tolerant; Orthodoxy + **Byzantine-style icons** continued), but nobles adopted Mongol dress and picked up **ideas of authoritarian rule**.
- Peasants fleeing attacks became **serfs of powerful landowners → rise of Russian serfdom** (lasted centuries).
- **Moscow won big:** collected tribute for the Mongols, rebuilt, took over the region; **from the 1380s** Moscow's princes led the fight to expel the Mongols (margin: expulsion of Tatars, **1380**) → independent by mid-15th c.
- **Poland & Hungary:** devastated, but no Mongol state was set up. Poland became **the most tolerant state in Europe — hosted up to 3/4 of Europe's Jews**; Lithuania emerged as a new state.

## 6.3 Western Europe: Politics & Culture
**Politics (still decentralized):**
- **Philip II Augustus** (France, 1180–1223): hired **middle-class officials**, sent central officials outward, used taxes for **mercenary armies** = nucleus of a stronger monarchy.
- Spain: Christian victory **1212** reduced Islam to **Granada**; **Castile + Aragon effectively united (textbook: 1375)** → Spain & Portugal began **sponsoring Atlantic exploration along Africa**.
- **1215 — Magna Carta** (King John): feudal rights vs. the king; mutual limits & obligations.
- **1265 — first English Parliament** (Lords = nobles/church; Commons = wealthy townspeople); **kings needed Parliament's OK for new taxes**. Parliaments also appeared in France, Spain, Germany.
- **Germany** = Holy Roman Empire in name only (bishops/nobles/cities ruled); **Italy** = city-states (merchant councils; censuses, diplomats, art patronage) + Papal States.
- **Hundred Years' War, 1337–1453** (England vs. France, lands + feudal rights vs. national states).

**Culture:**
- **Gothic** architecture (pointed arches, flying buttresses); **80 new cathedrals in France 1180–1270**; Amiens (1220–1402) = tallest building in Europe.
- **Scholasticism** + **Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)**: Summas, University of Paris; **faith first, but reason can know the natural order, moral law, even God** — parallels Islamic debates (al-Ghazali!), fed by translated Middle Eastern texts.
- **Roger Bacon** (Oxford): optics, building on Muslim scholars → **invention of eyeglasses**.
- Literature goes secular/vernacular: **Chaucer** (*Canterbury Tales* — "father of English literature"), **François Villon** (criminal/earthly themes, c. 1450), courtly-love ballads by **Christine de Pisan** (Italian woman writing in France).
- **Renaissance begins in Italy c. 1400:** classical revival, more secular. **Dante** (d. 1321, *Divine Comedy*), **Petrarch** (1304–1374; rediscovered **Cicero's letters**; humanist), **Giotto** (d. 1337; introduced **perspective** in painting).
- Popular piety: **veneration of Mary**, pilgrimages, magic; **St. Clare of Assisi** (fled home 1212, abbess at ~21, canonized 1255).
- Early church dissent: **John Wycliffe** (England — attacked clergy privilege, translated Bible into English) → **Jan Hus** (Czech; **burned at the stake 1415**). Foreshadows the Reformation after 1500.
- *Thinking Historically:* "Western civilization" is hard to define; postclassical Europeans would have recognized **Christendom** instead; Europe copied heavily from Asia/Arabs (math, commercial law, **paper — first European paper factory in Sicily, late 13th c.**).

## 6.4 Economy and Society
- **Origins of capitalism** by the 14th c.: Italian/German bankers + long-distance merchants investing for profit (**returns of 100%+ possible**); London merchants pooled money in **shared ventures**.
- **Jacques Coeur** (c. 1395–1456): French merchant — traded with Damascus (rugs, Chinese silk, Indonesian spices), financial advisor to the king, ennobled, **16-year-old son made archbishop**, then tortured and ruined by noble enemies → capitalism's risks.
- Weak governments → merchants unusually **free** (own commercial law, city courts); kings allied with merchants vs. nobles.
- **Guilds** dominated cities: group security, NOT profit; limited membership, regulated apprenticeships, guaranteed quality; real political voice. *(Europe's artisan edge: clocks.)*
- Rural escape rule: **a serf living in a city "a year and a day" became free**.
- Women: **veneration of Mary** gave cultural prestige; less segregated than under Islam (but couldn't lead services); marriage age rose.

**The 14th-c. crisis:**
- **Black Death** (bubonic plague) **from 1347**, via Middle East trading contacts; killed **up to 1/3 of Europe** (some estimates higher).
- Effects: religious fervor **+ persecution of Jews**; **labor shortage → wage demands**; landlords pushed back → **peasant revolts: France 1358, Britain 1381** ("in the days of Adam and Eve, there was no noble class" — egalitarian protests).
- Long-term: **serfdom loosened** (money rent instead of labor obligations); **later marriage (late 20s) → the nuclear "European-style family"** (few grandparents alive, strict control of young adults).

## Global Connections
- Western Europe (never invaded) **benefited from the Mongol network**: **Marco Polo** (set out 1273, reached China 2 yrs later) admired Chinese urban life + political structure; his travel book sparked European curiosity.
- Europe picked up from Asia/Arabs: **printing (→ typesetting by 1450), explosive powder (cannons by late 1300s, then guns), compass, improved sailing ships, paper, Arabic numerals, sugar/spices, commercial law**.
- **Mongol decline + Ottoman rise (Muslims, distrusted) closed the land routes → pushed Europe toward sea routes** = setup for the age of exploration after 1450.

## Quick self-test
1. What technically-named crusade destroyed a Christian city in 1204? → **Fourth Crusade (sack of Constantinople)**
2. Year Byzantium ended, and by whom? → **1453, Ottoman capture of Constantinople**
3. What did Mongol-era Russia gain vs. lose? → **Gained: Moscow's rise, Central Asia trade; Lost: trade with the West, peasant freedom (serfdom), some independence**
4. Why are they called parliaments and what was their key power? → **Bodies of privileged groups; approval of NEW TAXES (first: England 1265)**
5. What did Aquinas say about faith and reason? → **Faith always first, but reason can know the natural order, moral law, God**
6. Name the church critics. → **Wycliffe (England, English Bible) and Jan Hus (burned 1415)**
7. What economic ethic did guilds promote, vs. what new ethic? → **Guilds = group security/equality; capitalism = profit-seeking investment (14th c. origins)**
8. Two concrete results of the Black Death? → **Loosened serfdom (money rent) + late marriage → nuclear European-style family; also peasant revolts 1358/1381**
9. What technologies did Europe get from Asia via Mongol contacts? → **Printing, gunpowder weapons, compass, paper, Arabic numerals**
10. What pushed Europe toward the sea after 1450? → **Mongol collapse (land routes died) + Ottoman (Muslim) control of Middle East routes**
