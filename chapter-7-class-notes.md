## 7.1 African Societies: Diversity and Similarities
- sub-Saharan Africa = world's **2nd largest continent**, super diverse
- **NO universal states + NO universal religions** (unlike Asia/Europe/N. Africa) — but Christianity then Islam still found adherents
- **stateless societies** — (organized around kinship or age sets; council of families governs; NO taxes/rulers/army)
  - sometimes LARGER than neighboring states
  - dissidents could just leave → found new village (sparse population = safety valve)
- **secret societies** — (W. African forest; men AND women; controlled customs/beliefs; limited rulers' power) → alternative to state institutions
- **common elements across Africa:**
  - **Bantu migration** → shared language base (neighboring Bantu speakers could understand each other)
  - **animism** — (belief a soul/spirit exists in every object, even inanimate)
  - **diviners/priests** → combat evil/witchcraft, guide religious practice
  - **creator deity** + spirits + **founding ancestors** = "owners" of the land → ensure fertility of land/game/people
  - religion = **cosmology** (view of how universe works) + guide to ethics
- **pop. @ 1500 = 30–60 million** (guesswork — no censuses before mid-1700s)
- Islam in N. Africa appealed to kings (unites state + religion in one ruler = caliph) + **umma** — (community of believers, all EQUAL in law) → put Berbers on equal footing w/ Arabs
  - BUT practice ≠ ideal: social stratification stayed; **fine for killing a man = 2× fine for killing a woman**

### 7.1.4 The Christian Kingdoms: Nubia + Ethiopia
- Christianity reached Egypt + Ethiopia **BEFORE Rome converted** (4th c.)
- **Copts** — (Egyptian Christians) kept faith under Muslim rule (recognized as "people of the book")
- **Nubia** — (ancient Kush, up the Nile) resisted Muslims in the 9th c. → independent Christian kingdoms **until the 13th c.**
- **Ethiopia:** cut off by Muslim conquests → turned inward; highlands, fortified towns, **terraced hillsides**
  - **King Lalibela (d. 1221)** → **11 churches cut DOWN INTO THE ROCK** (Fig. 7.2: **Bet Giorgis**, cross-shaped roof at ground level, St. George = patron saint of Ethiopia)
  - dynasty traced to **Solomon + Sheba** (biblical marriage)
  - **Ge'ez** = religious language (from Axum) / **Amharic** = common speech
  - Muslim state + **Ottoman Turks** threatened Ethiopia → **Portuguese expedition @ 1542 landed at Massawa** → saved Christian kingdom
  - Portuguese tried to convert Ethiopia to Catholicism → FAILED → Ethiopia stayed isolated, Christian, independent

## 7.2 Kingdoms of the Grasslands (the Sahel)
- **Sahel** — (northern grassland belt of sub-Saharan Africa, just south of the desert)
- pattern: **Ghana (@ 8th c.) → Mali → Songhay + Hausa states** = African adaptations of Islam fused w/ local traditions
- **KEY ECONOMY: gold (from W. African forests) ↔ salt (from Sahara)** — camels introduced to Sahara @ 100–200 CE made crossings possible
- Sahel location = trade wealth BUT open plains → **attack + periodic droughts**
- **Sudanic states** = **conquest states** — (core ethnic/linguistic area ruling subordinate peoples via taxes, tribute, military)
  - rulers **sacred**, surrounded by ritual
  - **Islam = "royal cult"** → reinforced indigenous kingship; most of population NEVER converted
- **juula** — (Malinke merchants; small partnerships; spread trade throughout Mali + W. Africa)

### 7.2.2 Empire of Mali + Sundiata
- **Sundiata** — ("Lion Prince," **Keita clan**) emerged from exile/fighting → unified Mali; **died @ c. 1260**
  - created basic rules of Malinke society ("divided up the world") → became **mansa** — (emperor)
  - **federated provinces** + garrisons; travel secure; crime severely punished (per **Ibn Battuta**, 1304–1368)
- **griots** — (professional oral historians/bards; keepers of traditions; advisors to kings) → preserved the **Epic of Sundiata** orally for centuries (Document box: Sundiata vs. Sumaworo, evil sorcerer king of the Sosos)
- **Mansa Kankan Musa (r. 1312–1337):** @ 1324 hajj → Cairo → gave away SO much gold that **the price of gold FELL** (chronicler **al-Omari** visited Cairo ~12 yrs later)
- **Timbuktu:** pop. **50,000**, on the great bend of the **Niger River**
  - **Sankore mosque (@ 1324, built by Mansa Musa)** → library + **university**
  - **the book trade = the most lucrative business** (book = symbol of civilization in Islamic world)
- villages = **hoe farmers** (plows RARELY used) → rice (river valleys), millet, sorghums; farms rarely over **10 acres**; clearing land done communally

### 7.2.4 The Songhay Kingdom
- independent kingdom @ **7th c.**, maybe under a **Berber dynasty**; capital **Gao** (@ 1010; rulers Muslim, people mostly pagan)
- independent of Mali by the **1370s** → thrived on NEW gold sources from W. African forests
- **Sunni Ali (r. 1464–1492):** cavalry expanded borders → **seized Timbuktu + Djenne**; provincial administration; **PERSECUTED the Muslim scholars of Timbuktu**
- **Muhammad the Great** — (succeeded Sunni Ali; took military title **askia**) → extended Songhay's boundaries (mid-1500s)
- **@ 1591 — Moroccan army w/ MUSKETS crossed the Sahara → defeated the VASTLY LARGER Songhay forces** → revolts → empire broke apart
- **Hausa states** — (N. Nigeria, after Songhay's fall; e.g. **Kano**; combined Muslim + pagan traditions; fortified capitals, strong cavalry; traded salt, grains, cloth)
- **slavery:** marginal in Africa before Islam → trans-Saharan caravans carried slaves + gold
  - slaves = soldiers/administrators (no local ties = "trustworthy"), eunuchs, concubines → emphasis on enslaving women + children
  - **est. 4.8 million trans-Saharan + 2.4 million to Indian Ocean ports, over ~700 years**
  - children of slave mothers often freed → constant demand for MORE slaves
- **W. African mosques = clay/mud + wood beams** (Dogon mosques; Sankoré) → proves Islam arrived **gradually, via merchants/traders**, NOT conquest

## 7.3 The Swahili Coast of East Africa
- **Swahili** — (means "coastal"; **Bantu language + Arabic influence**, written in Arabic script before the 13th c.)
- by **13th c.** = string of trading ports: **Mogadishu, Mombasa, Malindi, Kilwa, Pate, Zanzibar** (~**30 towns** total) — each ruled by SEPARATE Muslim ruling families
- mosques/tombs/palaces of **cut stone + CORAL** (Mogadishu mosque @ 1231)
- ruling families claimed descent from immigrants from **Shiraz (Persia)** → legitimize their rule
- **EXPORTS: ivory, gold, iron, slaves, exotic animals** ↔ **IMPORTS: silks from Persia + porcelain from China**
- **Ibn Battuta** called **Kilwa** "one of the most beautiful and well constructed towns in the world"
- **Kilwa's 2 advantages:** (1) access to interior gold; (2) **furthest-south port ships from India could reach + return in a SINGLE monsoon season**
- **Chinese state expeditions stopped @ E. African coast in 1417 + 1431** (ivory, gold, rare woods) → China discontinued contact after 1431 → later Chinese goods came in Arab/Indian ships
- Islam barely spread inland; common people = **mud + thatch** houses surrounding Muslim elite's stone buildings → genuine hybrid culture
- @ c. 1500 Portuguese arrive → Kilwa declines, **Malindi + Mombasa** take over; Portuguese built **Fort Jesus @ Mombasa (1592)** but never controlled the N. coast

## 7.4 Peoples of the Forest and Plains
- non-Islamic trajectories: varied agriculture + herding; **iron tools/weapons**; mostly preliterate (oral transmission — NOT a measure of "civilization")

### 7.4.1 Artists and Kings: Yoruba + Benin
- **Yoruba** — (spoke a NON-Bantu language; small city-states, each ~50-mile radius; highly URBANIZED)
  - kings considered **divine**; huge royal court (secondary wives, musicians, magicians, soldier-slave bodyguards)
  - **Ile-Ife** = Yoruba culture flourishing @ 1000 (timeline); famous **terra-cotta + bronze portrait heads** (post-1200) = among greatest achievements of African art
- **Oyo** (Yoruba state, @ 14th c.): king = **alafin** → ruled provinces through "princes" (paid tribute)
  - **council of state** (nobles of the 7 city districts) LIMITED the king + **Ogboni** — (secret society of religious/political leaders) reviewed his decisions
- **Benin** — (Edo peoples, @ 14th c., present-day Nigeria)
  - **Ewuare the Great (r. 1440–1473)** → control from **Niger River to the sea near modern Lagos**
  - ruler = **oba** — (lived in a huge royal compound; authority buttressed by ritual)
  - famous **ivory + cast bronze** art; Portuguese arrived **1480s** → Benin bronzes began depicting Portuguese soldiers (Fig. 7.4: helmeted musketeers = marks of the Oba's power)

### 7.4.2 Kingdoms of Kongo + Mwene Mutapa
- **Kongo** — (Bantu kingdom on the LOWER CONGO RIVER; forming @ 13th c., flourishing by late 15th c.)
  - capital **Mbanza Kongo** = pop. **60,000–100,000** by early 1500s
  - ruler = **manikongo** — ("mani" = **BLACKSMITH** → iron = political + ritual power)
  - kingship **hereditary** BUT local chieftainships NOT → central authority could control subordinates; **8 major provinces**
  - sharp gender division of labor: men = clearing forest, palm oil/wine, hunting, long-distance trade / women = cultivation, livestock, **made salt from seawater + collected SEASHELLS = CURRENCY**
- **Great Zimbabwe** — (Shona-speaking Bantu confederation between the **Zambezi + Limpopo rivers**; from the 9th c.)
  - **~200 "zimbabwe" (stone house) sites** found; the largest = Great Zimbabwe
  - walls **15 ft thick, 30 ft high**; large conical tower; **built WITHOUT MORTAR**
  - religious symbol = **bird of God** (eagle = link between world + spirits)
  - stone building by **11th c.**; most sophisticated building @ **14th–15th c.**
  - 19th-c. Europeans wrongly credited Phoenicians/Arabs (prejudice) — archaeology proved **Bantu construction**
  - 15th c. → centralized state; king took title **Mwene Mutapa** (Portuguese said "Monomotapa")
  - controlled interior **gold** → traded via Arab port of **Sofala** (glass beads + porcelain found at the site)
  - split apart @ 16th c. (cattle wealth → soil exhaustion)

## Global Connections
- Africa NEVER had direct contact w/ the Mongols (products reached Mongols only **via Arab traders**) → no military/political rethinking like Asia/Europe
- 2 themes: internal state-building (Zimbabwe, Kongo, Benin, Yoruba = independent of Islam) + Islam's fusion w/ African cultures (Sahel + Swahili coast)
- **@ late 15th c. Portuguese arrive → found well-developed kingdoms able to deal w/ them as EQUALS**

## Thinking Historically — Two Demographic Transitions (margin terms!)
- **demography** — (the study of population); first estimate w/ <20% margin of error = **1750**
- **1st transition:** agriculture (Neolithic, after ~8000 BCE) → faster but still slow growth (~300M @ 1 CE → ~500M @ 1750)
- **2nd transition:** mid-1600s–1700s, tied to the **Industrial Revolution** → mortality falls (medicine, hygiene, diet) → then fertility falls
- **demographic transition** — (shift to low birth rate, low infant death rate, stable population; first in W. Europe/US, late 19th c.)
- **Ansley Coale (1970s):** current ~2%/yr growth = **100× the rate for most of human history** → called it transitory
