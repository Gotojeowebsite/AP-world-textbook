## Timeline anchors (margins)
**100–200 camels reach the Sahara** → **600–700 Islam spreads across North Africa** → **1000 Ghana at its height + Almoravid movement in the Sahara** → **1200 rise of Mali** → **1260 Sundiata dies** → **1300 Mali at its height (Kanem Empire = rival)** → **1324 Mansa Musa's hajj** → **1400 Timbuktu & Djenne flourish** → **1417 & 1431 last Chinese trade voyages to East Africa** → **1500 Songhay flourishes / Benin at its height** → **c. 1400–1550 Great Zimbabwe at its height**.

- **Almoravids** = Islamic reform movement in the Sahara (timeline margin, ~1000s) — worth knowing the name.

## Intro: Mansa Musa, 1324
- **Mansa Musa** (king of **Mali**) made the **hajj** with 100+ camels, distributing so much **gold in Cairo** that **the price of gold declined** (reported by chronicler **al-Omari**, who visited Cairo ~12 years later).
- Mali = **Malinke** peoples, savanna state between desert and forest; rose on **gold + control of caravan routes**; powerful army, empire **Niger → Senegal rivers**.
- **Ibn Battuta** visited Mali after the pilgrimage: found local customs less refined than elegant courts but admired Mali's justice ("of all peoples, the Blacks most hate injustice").
- Key point: sub-Saharan Africa was **never totally isolated**; Islam arrived **after 800 CE** and transformed some societies, but others kept their own trajectories. **No direct Mongol contact** — Africa's products reached the Mongols only via Arab traders.

## 7.1 African Societies: Diversity & Similarities
- No **universal states** or universal religions in sub-Saharan Africa — but Christianity (Nubia, Ethiopia) and Islam shaped some regions.
- **Stateless societies:** organized around **kinship or age sets**; governed by councils of families — no taxes/rulers. Sometimes **larger than neighboring states**. Dissidents could just leave and found a new village (sparsely populated continent).
- **Secret societies** (West African forest): men & women controlling customs/beliefs, limiting rulers — alternative to state institutions.
- **Common elements:** **Bantu language base** (mutual understanding across regions); **animism** (spirit in every object); **diviners/priests** vs. witchcraft; **creator deity + ancestral spirits** (ancestors = "owners" of the land); religion, economics, history intertwined.
- **Population by 1500: 30–60 million** (estimates are shaky — no censuses before the mid-1700s).

**Thinking Historically — Two Demographic Transitions (margin terms!):**
- **Demography** = the study of population. First estimate with <20% margin of error: **1750**.
- **First transition:** agriculture (Neolithic, after ~8000 BCE) → faster but still slow growth (world ~300M at 1 CE → ~500M by 1750).
- **Second transition:** mid-1600s–1700s, tied to the **Industrial Revolution** — mortality falls (medicine, hygiene, diet), then fertility falls → **demographic transition = shift to low birth rate, low infant death rate, stable population** (first in Western Europe/US, late 19th c.).
- **Ansley Coale (1970s):** current ~2%/yr growth = **100× the rate for most of human history** — he called the present growth spurt transitory.

**7.1.3 Islam in North Africa:**
- Islam appealed to kings (unites state + religion in one ruler) and converts (**umma** = equality of all believers, put Berbers on equal legal footing with Arabs).
- But practice lagged: stratification persisted; **fine for killing a man was twice that for killing a woman** → gap between ideal and practice fueled utopian reform movements.

**7.1.4 Christian Kingdoms: Nubia & Ethiopia:**
- Christianity reached **Egypt & Ethiopia BEFORE Rome converted (4th c.)**. **Copts** kept their faith under Muslim rule; **Nubia** (ancient Kush) resisted Muslim penetration in the 9th c. → independent Christian kingdoms **until the 13th century**.
- **Ethiopia:** cut off by Muslim conquests, turned inward; highlands, fortified towns, **terraced hillsides**.
- **King Lalibela (d. 1221):** 11 great churches **cut down into the rock** at Lalibela (incl. **Bet Giorgis**, cross-shaped roof at ground level, dedicated to St. George).
- 13th–14th c. dynasty traced origins to **Solomon and Sheba**; used **Ge'ez** (religious language of Axum) + **Amharic** (common speech).
- When a Muslim state + **Ottoman Turks** threatened Ethiopia, a **Portuguese expedition landed at Massawa in 1542** and saved it → Ethiopia stayed isolated, Christian, independent (Portuguese bid to convert it to Catholicism failed).

## 7.2 Kingdoms of the Grasslands (Sahel)
- Pattern: **Ghana (by 8th c.) → Mali → Songhay + Hausa states** = African adaptations of Islam fused with local tradition.
- Built on **trans-Saharan trade**: **gold from West African forests exchanged for SALT** (and dates/Mediterranean goods). **Camels introduced to the Sahara 100–200 CE** made the crossings possible.
- Sahel position = trade wealth BUT **exposure to attack + drought**.
- **Sudanic states = conquest states**: a core ethnic/linguistic area ruling subordinate peoples via taxes/tribute. Rulers **sacred**; **Islam = "royal cult"** reinforcing kingship — most of the population never converted.
- **Juula** = Malinke merchants spreading trade across Mali and West Africa.

**Mali & Sundiata:**
- **Sundiata ("Lion Prince," Keita clan)** unified Mali after exile + wars vs. the Soso king **Sumaworo**; died **about 1260**; credited with creating Malinke society's basic rules ("divided up the world") → became **mansa** (emperor).
- Government: **federated provinces** + **garrisons**; secure travel; harsh punishment of crime (per Ibn Battuta, 1304–1368).
- **Griots** = professional oral historians/bards, keepers of tradition and advisors to kings (the **Epic of Sundiata** — Sundiata vs. the sorcerer Sumaworo — passed down orally for centuries).
- **Mansa Kankan Musa (r. 1312–1337):** the famous hajj; brought Muslim world's attention to Mali.
- **Timbuktu:** population **50,000**; on the Niger bend; **Sankore mosque** (begun **1324 by Mansa Musa**) with a **library + university** — **the book trade was Timbuktu's most lucrative business**.
- Villages: **hoe farming** (plows rarely used) — rice in river valleys, millet, sorghum; farms rarely over **10 acres**.

**Songhay:**
- Formed as an independent kingdom **7th century**, possibly under a **Berber dynasty**; capital at **Gao** (by **1010**; rulers Muslim, people mostly pagan).
- Independent from Mali by the **1370s**; thrived on new **gold from West African forests**.
- **Sunni Ali (r. 1464–1492):** cavalry expanded the empire; **seized Timbuktu and Djenne**; provincial administration; **persecuted the Muslim scholars of Timbuktu**.
- Succeeded by **Muhammad the Great** (took the military title **askia**) — extended Songhay's boundaries, mid-16th c.
- **1591 — Moroccan army with MUSKETS crossed the Sahara and defeated the much larger Songhay forces** → revolts, empire broke apart. (Hausa states like **Kano** rose after Songhay's demise.)

**Political/social life in the Sudanic states:**
- Rulers used Islamic titles (**emir, caliph**) + literate Muslim advisors/scribes; state formation **heightened social hierarchy**.
- Islam **accommodated pagan practice** — rulers like Sundiata & Sunni Ali stayed tied to local spirits.
- **Slavery:** pre-Islamic African slavery was marginal; Islamic trade made it big — caravans carried **slaves + gold**; slaves = soldiers/administrators (no local ties = "trustworthy"), eunuchs, concubines. **Trans-Saharan slave trade estimate: 4.8 million + 2.4 million to Indian Ocean ports, over ~700 years.** Children of slave mothers were often freed → constant demand for replacements.
- **West African mosques:** clay/mud + wood beams (Dogon mosques; Sankoré) — local style shows Islam arrived **gradually, via merchants/traders**, not conquest.

## 7.3 The Swahili Coast
- **By the 13th century** a string of trading ports: **Mogadishu, Mombasa, Malindi, Kilwa, Pate, Zanzibar** (about **30 towns** eventually). **Swahili = "coastal"** — **Bantu language + Arabic influence**, written in Arabic script before the 13th c.; separate **Muslim ruling families**.
- Stone-and-**coral** mosques/tombs/palaces (Mogadishu mosque begun **1231**). Ruling families claimed descent from **Shiraz (Persia)** to legitimize rule.
- **Exports: ivory, gold, iron, slaves, exotic animals** ↔ **Persian silks + Chinese porcelain**.
- **Ibn Battuta called Kilwa "one of the most beautiful and well constructed towns in the world."** Kilwa's edge: access to interior gold + **the farthest-south port ships from India could reach and return in a single monsoon season**.
- **Chinese fleet voyages (Zheng He's state expeditions) stopped at East Africa in 1417 and 1431** (ivory, gold, rare woods) — then China withdrew; later trade went in Arab/Indian ships.
- Islam barely penetrated the interior; common people lived in **mud & thatch** around the Muslim elite's stone buildings — a genuine **hybrid culture**.
- By **c. 1500**: Portuguese arrive; Kilwa declined, **Malindi & Mombasa** took over; Portuguese built **Fort Jesus at Mombasa in 1592** but never controlled the northern coast.

## 7.4 Peoples of the Forest and Plains
- Non-Islamic trajectories: agriculture + **iron tools**; preliterate (oral transmission ≠ less civilized).

**Yoruba & Benin:**
- **Yoruba** (non-Bantu language): **city-states** (each ~50-mile radius), **divine kings**, huge royal courts. **Ile-Ife** = flourishing Yoruba culture center; famous **terra-cotta & bronze portrait heads** (post-1200).
- **Oyo** (by the 14th c.): king = **alafin**; ruled via **"princes"** paying tribute; **council of state** (nobles of the 7 city districts) limited him; **Ogboni** secret society reviewed decisions.
- **Benin** (Edo peoples, 14th c.): under **Ewuare the Great (r. 1440–1473)** stretched from the **Niger River to the sea near Lagos**. Ruler = **oba**; famous **ivory & cast bronze** art; Portuguese arrived **1480s** and were impressed — Benin bronzes began depicting Portuguese soldiers.

**Kongo:**
- Bantu kingdom on the **lower Congo River, from the 13th c.**; flourishing by the late 15th c.
- Capital **Mbanza Kongo** (60,000–100,000 people by early 1500s); ruled by the **manikongo** ("mani" = **blacksmith** — iron = power); **hereditary kingship vs. non-hereditary chieftainships**; **8 provinces**.
- Strict gender division of labor: men = clearing forest, palm oil/wine, hunting, long-distance trade; women = **cultivation, livestock, salt from seawater, collecting seashells = CURRENCY**.

**Great Zimbabwe / Mwene Mutapa:**
- **Shona-speaking peoples** between the **Zambezi and Limpopo rivers**; from the **9th century** built stone courts — **~200 "zimbabwe" (stone house) sites** found.
- **Great Zimbabwe:** walls **15 feet thick, 30 feet high**, conical tower, **no mortar**; linked to the **bird of God (eagle)** symbol; Bantu construction began in stone by the **11th century**, peak building **14th–15th centuries** (19th-c. Europeans wrongly credited Phoenicians/Arabs — prejudice).
- 15th c.: centralized state under the **Mwene Mutapa** (Portuguese said "Monomotapa"); controlled interior **gold** sources, traded via the Arab port of **Sofala** (glass beads + porcelain found at the site); split apart in the 16th c. (cattle wealth → soil exhaustion).

## Global Connections
- Africa intensified contacts with **North Africa + the Middle East**, but **never directly with the Mongols** — no rethinking of military/political patterns like Asia/Europe faced.
- Two big themes: **internal state-building** (Zimbabwe, Kongo, Benin, Yoruba = independent of Islam) + **Islam's fusion with African cultures** (Sudanic states, Swahili coast).
- When the **Portuguese arrived c. 1480s**, they found **well-developed kingdoms able to deal with them as equals** — the raw deal comes later.

## Quick self-test
1. What economic effect did Mansa Musa's 1324 caravan have in Cairo? → **So much gold was spent that gold's price declined**
2. Name the Mali founder, his clan, his title, and his death year. → **Sundiata, Keita clan, mansa, c. 1260**
3. What made Timbuktu a center of learning? → **Sankore mosque (1324) with library + university; book trade = most lucrative business**
4. Who destroyed Songhay and when — be specific. → **A Moroccan army with muskets, 1591**
5. Why was Kilwa so well positioned? → **Interior gold access + farthest-south port for a single-monsoon round trip from India**
6. What languages/roots make up Swahili? → **Bantu base + Arabic vocabulary, written in Arabic script**
7. Who built Great Zimbabwe, and how do we know? → **Shona-speaking Bantus (stone from the 11th c., peak 14th–15th); archaeology disproved the Phoenician/Arab myth**
8. What was the currency of Kongo, and who collected it? → **Seashells, collected by women**
9. How did Islam reinforce kingship in the Sahel? → **Royal cult uniting civil + religious authority (emir/caliph titles) layered on sacred African kingship; most people stayed unconverted**
10. Who arrived in Ethiopia in 1542 and why? → **Portuguese expedition at Massawa, to save the Christian kingdom from a Muslim state backed by the Ottomans**
